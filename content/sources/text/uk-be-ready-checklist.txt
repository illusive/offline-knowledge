BE READY CHECKLIST
Being ready for 3 days helps you manage while support reaches those
most in need.
Doing this is easier than you might think. You could have some of the
items at home already.
You don’t need to do it all at once. Build up your readiness gradually over
time by taking it step by step, and picking up an extra item or two when
you can.
How to get started:
● See what you already have: Check your cupboards first to see what
essentials you already keep at home.
● Plan for your household: Note down what you still need, remembering
items your family relies on, such as medical supplies, baby formula or
pet food.
● Build it gradually: Pick up missing items when you can during your
regular shopping trips to help spread the cost.
● Check it regularly: Set a reminder every six months to check
expiry dates and swap out items that need replacing, or update your kit
if your household’s needs have changed.
● Connect with your community: Talk to your neighbours about what
extra support you might be able to share during an outage, and swap
contact details.

Use the checklist below to review the steps you have already taken, and what
you might still need to do.
Light and warmth
● Torch (and spare
batteries).
● Blankets, sleeping bags
and warm clothing.
Communication and
information
● FM radio (battery/
wind-up).
● Charged power bank.
Money and
documents
● Cash (mix of coins
and notes) and cards.
● Copies of IDs/documents.
● Important phone
numbers and information
(e.g. regular medication
and doses).
Medical and health
● First aid kit / first
aid items.
● Essential medication
and medical supplies.
Baby products
● Baby formula.
Ready-made or ‘ready-to-
feed’ formula is best,
as you may not be able
to boil water to make
up powdered
formula safely.
● Nappies and wipes.
Pet products
● Pet food and
bottled water.
See what simple steps you can take at GOV.UK/BeReady
Food
● Non-perishable food
for 3 days.
Food with a long shelf life
that doesn’t need
refrigeration or cooking
e.g. tinned meat, fish or
vegetables, dried fruit and
nuts. It’s best to buy food
you’d usually eat.
Hygiene
● Hand sanitiser, wet
wipes, hygiene and
bathroom products.
Water
● Bottled water for 3 days.
3 litres per person per
day for drinking. More
would be needed for other
tasks such as cooking
and washing.
If you have any extra items, you could consider donating these to others who might
not have the means to prepare their own household. You may be able to help
someone by photocopying or printing important documents for them.
